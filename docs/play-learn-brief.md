# PLAY & LEARN — the original games brief (owner's chosen design, Nov 11, 2026)

This is the brief the six games were designed from. It was delivered to the build session in two messages; the second (levels/sound/fun addendum) arrived, the first did not. Reconcile the shipped games against BOTH parts.

## Part 1 — the games

Your owner has chosen the games himself, from a list of twelve: **Word Snake, Word Catch, The Ladder, Memory Match, Duel Dash — plus the Journey Map over all of them.** Build them exactly, and build them to be loved: a **Play & Learn wing — "Ciyaar oo Baro"** — inside the academy, for visual learners, so full of life that students stay, play, challenge each other, level up… and learn English without feeling the work.

Who it's for, always: Somali learners — elders, parents, the family's kids — many on older phones. Every game is visual first (pictures, motion, color, sound), playable by someone who cannot yet read much English, and fast on a cheap phone. Every game drinks from the academy's own well: the real lesson vocabulary and sentences already in this app — never random word lists; what she plays is what her lessons test. Every missed word quietly enters her SRS review deck, and every win pays the app's real XP.

THE FIVE:

1. **WORD SNAKE** — the beloved classic, turned teacher: a picture (or Somali meaning) is shown; the snake eats letters scattered on the field to spell its English word, growing with each right letter. A wrong letter costs a life, never her dignity. This is spelling learned at arcade speed.
2. **WORD CATCH** — words rain over a scene; the picture/meaning at the top is the target; she slides her basket to catch only the matching word and dodge the rest. Gentle parallax depth — a 3D feel in feather-light code. Speed rises as she levels.
3. **THE LADDER** — quiz stairs with rising stakes: each correct answer climbs a step for more XP; at each landing she may BANK her XP or risk the climb; two lifelines per game (remove two wrong answers; hear the question spoken slowly). One wrong step ends the climb — banked XP is safe, unbanked is lost. Make the tension delicious and the banking ceremony satisfying.
4. **MEMORY MATCH** — the calm one, the elders' morning game: flip cards — picture ↔ English word, word ↔ Somali meaning. Cleared pairs speak the word aloud. No clock on Easy; a gentle one above. Finishing a full board earns a completion moment worth seeing.
5. **DUEL DASH** — 60-second head-to-head speed quiz, the rivalry engine. Two ways, both honest (this app has no server — never fake "online"): (a) pass-and-play on one phone, two players alternating rounds — mother vs daughter, elder vs grandchild; (b) challenge by code — she plays her round, the app mints a short code/link carrying the exact questions and her score, she sends it by WhatsApp, her challenger plays the identical round and races her score. Add the **family leaderboard**: the profiles already living on this phone, ranked by game XP — the household table everyone wants to top. And in your report, one short honest note: what real-time online duels would need (accounts + a small backend — the Phase 4 road), so the owner chooses later with open eyes.

THE GLUE — **THE JOURNEY MAP**: over all five games, a visible path she travels as she levels up — each level a place on the journey, each arrival a small ceremony; daily quests (three small missions a day: one game, one review, one new word set) with a treasure stop when all three land. This map is the habit engine: it is what turns five minutes into every day. Show it on the Play & Learn home and echo today's quest on the app Home.

DIFFICULTY: every game ships Easy / Medium / Hard (Xad fudud / Dhexe / Adag) — speed, distractors, word level and lives change; the kindness never does. Difficulty is chosen per game and remembered per player profile.

SOUND + PICTURES: the device's voices speak the words (English clear, slow on request); pictures in the app's own art language — icons, scenes, color — no heavy downloads, no engine that makes an elder's phone crawl. Rich in feel, light in weight: if it doesn't run beautifully on a five-year-old phone, redesign it until it does.

PLACEMENT: Play & Learn gets its own proud entrance — its own page in the app's navigation family, never layered over lessons. Lessons stay clean; the games floor is a door she chooses to open.

Guard the sacred list as always: her progress and the `zamzam-academy` database untouched, lesson order and unlocking preserved, Somali the only support language, no YouTube-side captions, the Phase 1/2 design language — teal, paper, sun gold — native, never bolted on, never AI slop.

Finish like an owner: play every game yourself at phone size, Easy and Hard; run a full pass-and-play duel and a challenge code end to end; break them before a learner can. Then commit, push, and report: what you built per game, how difficulty, XP and the Journey Map work, how Duel Dash challenges work, and the honest online note.

## Part 2 — levels, sound, fun (addendum)

Owner's ruling on Play & Learn, in his words: **fun, fun, fun is the goal — fun and learn.** Three upgrades to the games brief, plus one more game:

1. **REAL LEVELS INSIDE EVERY GAME — 10 each.** Not just the Journey Map: Word Snake, Word Catch, The Ladder, Memory Match and Duel Dash each get their own 10-level ladder (Heer 1–10). Every level is a little harder and a little richer — faster falls, longer words, trickier distractors, fewer lives — and finishing one pays 1–3 stars and unlocks the next with a ceremony she can feel. The Journey Map shows her game levels as the places she has reached. A learner should always see the next level glowing one step ahead: that glow is the habit.
2. **SOUND — a games floor must be heard.** Give Play & Learn a real sound design, built with WebAudio so it weighs nothing and works offline: a happy blip when the snake eats a right letter, a soft wooden *tok* for a catch, a warm chime for correct, a gentle low thud (never a buzzer, never shame) for a miss, a rising fanfare for level-up, coins-and-sparkle for banking XP on The Ladder. Plus a light, cheerful music loop under the games home — and one big, friendly sound on/off button she can find without hunting, remembered per profile. Spoken words still use the device voices; sounds never talk over them.
3. **JUICE — fun is a feature, spec it.** Particles when a pair matches, a basket that wiggles with joy, combo streaks inside a round (×2, ×3 multipliers for back-to-back correct), near-miss slow-motion on the last life, confetti that behaves like it means it. Every celebration still carries a word — she hears and sees the English at the exact moment she feels the win. That pairing, joy + word, is the whole science of this floor.

AND GAME 6 — **WORD SLICE**: the most joyful touch mechanic on any phone. Words ride fruits and objects tossed into the air; the picture at the top names the target; she slices through the correct word with a finger swipe — juice, sparkle, a perfect *shwick* — and must NOT slice the wrong-word bombs. Combos for slicing two correct in one swipe. Easy is slow and forgiving; Hard is a fruit storm. Ten levels, same ladder law.

Everything else in the games brief stands — real curriculum words only, missed words to SRS, XP real, difficulties remembered, five-year-old phones purring, lessons untouched, sacred list guarded. Report the levels, sounds and juice per game when you ship it.
